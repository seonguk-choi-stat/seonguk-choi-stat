### Current Work: Wasserstein Gradient Descent Decompounding (WGDD)

WGDD recovers the law of the summands of a compound sum $S = X_1 + \dots + X_N$ from aggregate pairs $(N, S)$, such as claim counts and total losses in insurance or earner counts and household income in surveys.
It fits the summand law by minimizing the 2-Wasserstein distance between simulated and observed totals, using Wasserstein gradient steps on a quantile sieve, so it needs only the ability to simulate totals from a candidate law.

- Identifiability, strong consistency of the estimator, and convergence of the optimizer to Clarke-stationary points
- Extension to dependence between the count and the summands, and to published data that suppress small counts
- Methods paper in preparation with Prof. Jongmin Lee
